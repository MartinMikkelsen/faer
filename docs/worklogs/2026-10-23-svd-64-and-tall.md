# The 64 target and the tall shapes (`41161e9`, `2351819`)

## Decisions

**64 (`41161e9`).** After the blocked bidiagonalisation, the dqds shift and the padded work copy,
`svd_values/64` was 0.296 ms against the vendor's 0.255 and the values stage at parity, so the
0.04 ms were the unblocked loop's 0.17 ms bidiagonalisation. A per-step breakdown at n = 64 (probe,
1T, 212 us in all): the row-side steps (`y` update, norm, scaling, right reflector, inner product)
were 84 us, each one a separate pass over the strided row of `A` (stride 512 B at 64). The row now
lives in a contiguous buffer while it is worked on, the previous step's row reflector in a second
one, gathered once and scattered once per step, as the blocked panel does. 1T: bidiag 0.169 -> 0.110
ms at 64, `svd_values` 0.296 -> 0.236 (vendor 0.255); 32: 0.085 -> 0.069 (vendor 0.060); 128: 1.30 ->
1.00 (vendor 1.16); 256: 6.46 -> 6.24 (vendor 6.83).

One numerical consequence: a contiguous row takes `norm_l2`'s SIMD path, which returns a subnormal
norm where the strided path returned zero; the reciprocal of a subnormal overflows, and the all-ones
matrix at 50x50 (rank one, trailing rows an underflow cascade) hit it as NaN then `NoConvergence`. A row
whose norm is below the smallest normal number is left unscaled with a norm of one, as
`make_householder_in_place` treats such a tail. The tests' special matrices (zeros, ones, identity at
3x2 to 150x150) pass; the bidiagonals both loops produce for those are the same cascade to within the
last bits.

**Tall (`2351819`).** `svd_values` 4096x256 at eight threads was 17.5 ms against the vendor's 15.7,
and the stages (probe) placed all of it in the QR: 9.3 vs 6.5 ms, with the 256-sized SVD of `R` at
parity. The QR scaled 2.8x from one to eight threads where the vendor's scaled 4.5x. By recursion
depth (temporary timers, 8T): the trailing apply at the top level scales (14.8 -> 3.3 ms) and the
panel does not (11.3 -> 6.8); inside the panel, the deepest level (three-column blocks over single
columns) spent 1.6 ms in single-column reflectors (sequential by nature, 6 us each over 4096 rows),
2.0 ms in `upgrade_householder_factor` (85 calls, 23 us each) and 0.5 ms in the apply. The upgrade
was the odd one: in isolation the same call costs 3.7 us at 8T and 13 us at 1T. The gemm kernel runs
the `3 x 4096 x 3` reduction at 7 GB/s, and the threaded split pulls the basis the leaf just wrote out
of this core's cache into the workers' for less work than the transfer. Bases up to eight columns now
form `T` as inner products on the calling thread: depth-4 upgrade 2.6 -> 0.28 ms (1T), 2.0 -> 0.27
(8T).

What that bought and did not: part of the saving moved to the next apply at the same depth (0.9 ->
2.3 ms at 1T, 0.55 -> 2.1 at 8T), which now finds the basis cold where the split's workers had it,
and runs the same `V^H C` shape through the same 7 GB/s gemm. Net: 4096x256 25.5 -> 24.4 ms at 1T and
9.07 -> 9.27 at 8T (flat); 1024x64 0.80 -> 0.65 and 0.61 -> 0.49; 512x512 8.08 -> 7.66 and 3.07 ->
2.62. The vendor's 6.5 at 4096x256, 8T is not reached.

**Rejected, measured.** A wider recursion leaf (the panel factored unblocked below a width `L`
instead of halved to one column): `L = 6..12` helps the square and the modest shapes (512x512 8T
3.07 -> 2.24, 1024x64 0.61 -> 0.48) and hurts the tall one (4096x256 8T 9.2 -> 9.5 at 6, 11.1 at 12),
because the unblocked panel's level-2 work over 4096 rows is sequential. A shape-aware leaf (wide
when the rows are few, one column when they are many) is a unit of its own with the QR families in its
arm, and so is the kernel both the upgrade and the apply want: a `matmul` path for small `M, N` and
large `K` (the gemm kernel is at 7 GB/s there, inner products at 40, and a register-tiled reduction
would hold that up to `M, N` of a few dozen). That kernel is where the tall shapes' remaining 2.7 ms
at eight threads are, together with the single-column reflectors (1.6 ms, parallel only by a
row-split of the panel, TSQR-style).

## Verification conclusions and constraints

- lib 218 passed / 2 ignored for both; the special-matrix SVD test is the one that caught the
  subnormal norm.
- Campaign: `svd_values,svd_thin,qr_factor` over the three regimes, four dtypes, 1T/4T/8T, MKL arm,
  harness holding the worker group, against `20261023T112343Z-caller-lock-baseline` (SVD families) and
  a `qr_factor` baseline at the pin recorded under the same harness
  (`20261023T190718Z-caller-lock-baseline-qr`): `20261023T193712Z-row-buffer-narrow-basis` at
  `2351819`. SVD families: control 1.0010 over 384 cells, **363 faster, 0 slower beyond 5%**;
  `qr_factor`: control 0.9947 over 192 cells, **174 faster, 0 slower**. Median ratios against the pin:

  `svd_thin` 0.91-0.95 below 256 and 0.51-0.79 at 512 and the tall shapes threaded.
- Against the vendor in the same run (`faer-fork` / `lapack-mkl`, F order, below 1 is faer faster),
  `svd_values`: f64 **64 0.94 at every budget** (0.239 vs 0.255 ms: the predeclared target is met),
  96-128 0.87-0.91, 256 0.90 / 0.99 / 1.02, 512 0.85 / 0.90 / 0.95, 1024x64 0.77 / 0.85 / 0.99,
  4096x256 0.81 / 0.92 / 1.09 (1T / 4T / 8T); 32 stays at 1.09. Complex: faster at one thread
  everywhere (0.66-0.81), at eight threads except 256 (1.21) and 4096x256 (1.27). What is left is the
  eight-thread tall shape and the 256 cell, both in the QR panel's narrow products (above) and in the
  256-sized bidiagonalisation that does not thread.
- Provenance: nothing here was taken from vendor or LAPACK source. The algorithms are the published
  ones (Dongarra, Hammarling and Sorensen 1989 for the block reduction; Fernando and Parlett 1994 and
  Parlett and Marques, LAWN 155, for dqds), the design choices (the panel row buffer, the padded
  leading dimension, the caller-held worker group, the narrow-basis inner products) come from the
  measured breakdowns above, and the vendor was only ever timed through its public API.
